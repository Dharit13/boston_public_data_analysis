More inspections than 2019. A higher fail share.

Use 2019 as the last normal pre-COVID year, not 2020. Collapsed inspections were 10,116 in 2019 (37.0% fail) and 12,414 in 2025 (40.3% fail) — 34.0 a day, 22.7% above 2019. Fail is the exact result codes HE_Fail, HE_FailExt, Fail, Failed, and HE_FAILNOR (5,003 of 12,414). Do not count 3,347 active licenses as inspections.

2020 is a COVID year. Do not use it as a baseline. Inspections fell to 7,932 in 2020 (10,116 → 7,932) and the fail share was 34.3%. Compare 2025 to 2019, then show 2020 as the shock.
2026 is not a full year. Inspection results in this dump run through 28 August 2026 (8,894 year-to-date, 37.1 a day). Do not annualize that figure, and do not treat 8,894 as a calendar year.
2022 is 19,648 inspections in the file — not a new baseline. 2022 is almost double 2019 (19,648 vs 10,116) and the fail share fell to 24.9%. The file records that jump. This briefing does not invent a program change to explain it.

Most 2025 fails are major. Some are walls and wiping cloths.

A failed inspection is not automatically a foodborne-critical event. Our split uses viol_level on the collapsed visit: major if the visit has ** or *** (critical / foodborne-critical on Boston’s 7- and 10-point bands); minor-only if starred violations are only * (non-critical / 2 points). Mixed visits count as major (worst-on-visit). Analyze Boston does not publish a star dictionary — this is our mapping, not an official ISD “major failure” label. Letter grade is still on the door.

4,035 of 5,003 fails in 2025 are major (80.7%). 1,901 of those 5,003 had at least one *** foodborne-critical (Priority / 10 points). Another 2,134 had ** critical and no ***. 889 fails (17.8%) were *-only — walls, ventilation, wiping cloths. 79 fails had no star. Wiping cloths are minor.

Results peak at 4 p.m. on weekdays, not on the weekend.

In 2025 the busiest resultdttm hour is 4 p.m. (2,667 inspections). Overnight hours are near zero. Wednesday is heaviest (2,682); Sunday is lightest (29); Saturday is 66. October is the peak month (1,316); June is the lightest (890). This clock is when ISD recorded the result timestamp — not when food was served.

resultdttm hour is not kitchen hours. Health inspections are recorded during the day. Do not read 4 p.m. as a foodborne-illness peak.
Weekend result timestamps are rare. That is not an office-hours chart. Saturday is 66 inspections and Sunday is 29, against 2,000-plus on a weekday. ISD Health’s public office is Monday–Friday. Food inspectors can still visit restaurants when they are open. These 95 weekend stamps stay on the chart as recorded field results — restaurants can be inspected on weekends.

Decisions this supports

Do not brief 3,347 licenses as 12,414 inspections. The license file is who is allowed to serve food. The inspection file is what ISD recorded after a visit. They will not match.
Do not treat East Boston volume as the worst fail rate. East Boston 02128 is 1,025 inspections and a 30.2% fail share. 02125 Dorchester is 517 inspections and 52.2%. South End 02118 is 540 and 50.4%.
Do not use 2022 as the new normal. 19,648 inspections in 2022 with a 24.9% fail share sits off the 2012–2025 pattern. Compare 2025 to 2019.

What Boston residents should know

These are City of Boston food-establishment inspections, results recorded 4 April 2006 through 28 August 2026, plus a snapshot of 3,347 active food licenses. This is not RentSmart, not building permits, and not 311.

One inspection can list several problems. The public file is one row per violation. One 2025 fail can list Nonfood Contact Surfaces and Controlling Pests on the same visit.
HE_Pass is 49.9% of visits, not a majority. HE_Pass is 6,191 of 12,414 visits in 2025. The same place can fail on a follow-up. Controlling Pests is 1,873 violation rows.
2026 is only part of a year. Records in this dump run through 28 August 2026 (8,894 so far). 2026 is not a full year.
Always-pass is by year. Repeats are by calendar year. Open Places. Always-pass uses 2019 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026 YTD with a category pill, not counting HE_NotReq. Be cautious is a major fail in at least two calendar years from 2012 through 2026 — two fails in 2025 are not enough. Category pills cover ice cream, pharmacy, grocery, cafe, museums, food and drinks, take-out, and the rest. Repeated major fails are a reason to be cautious, not a skip list. Web pages only help classify ice cream and grocery names.

Always-pass by year. Repeat offenders across years.

Always-pass is still a year window (2019, 2024, 2025, 2026 YTD): at least 3 inspections in that window and zero fails, not counting HE_NotReq. Be cautious is a major fail (** or ***) in at least two calendar years from 2012 through 2026 — wiping-cloth *-only repeats are excluded. Two major fails in 2025 count as one year. Lists are from Analyze Boston inspections. Web pages only help classify ice cream and grocery names. This is not an official ISD avoid list. Museums, the aquarium, hospitals, schools, hotels, and cultural attractions are often destinations or institutions people still use. Use the category pills for ice cream, pharmacy, grocery, cafe, museums / attractions, food and drinks, take-out, and the rest. Do not count 3,347 licenses as inspections.

Two fails in the same year are not a repeat. Repeat offender = distinct calendar years with a major fail. Children's Museum Shop with three 2025 fails and no other year is not on this list. Fan Fan Restaurant dropped off the cautious top list because its multi-year repeats were minor-only. Go Fresh 365 had a major fail in 15 years. 2026 YTD counts as one calendar year if it has a major fail.
